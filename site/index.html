What this is

We are building a long-term evidence archive of documents, sanctions data, war reporting and other records about Russia's war against Ukraine and the networks that sustain it. The goal is a record that still holds up years or decades from now, for courts, investigators, journalists and historians as well as for us.

The project calls the war Ukraine's Second War of Independence. We chose that name on purpose, and we say so openly. The project is independent and not Ukrainian. It supports Ukrainian sovereignty. It also holds itself to strict standards of evidence, and it keeps clear lines between what a source claims, what the evidence shows, what we infer, and what a court has found.

The archive comes first and the website comes last. Everything we eventually publish will rest on material that has already been captured, checked and preserved.

The principles it is built on

  • Capture exactly and never alter. We keep every file exactly as it was received, with a fingerprint (a checksum) that shows whether it has changed since. The one exception is redaction to protect people, and every redaction is recorded.
  • A claim is not a fact. A sanctions listing, a military report or a UN count is kept as that source's claim. Figures from different sources are never averaged into one number.
  • People decide and software assists. Software can fetch and preserve material. Only a person can decide that something is established or publish it.
  • Failures are recorded, not hidden. A download that fails or a page that has disappeared becomes part of the record, with an explanation.
  • We are honest about limits. The archive does not claim a legal chain of custody. Where something has not been verified, we say so.
  • Every decision is written down. Each significant choice is recorded, with the options considered and the reasons for the choice. A recorded decision is never quietly edited. A new record replaces it instead.

How material moves through the archive

Every item passes the same checkpoints in the same order. Each checkpoint is a separate decision, so preserving an item does not mean it gets published.

  1. A source is registered Nothing is collected from a source until a person has checked it by hand and formally approved it, one source at a time. Human decision
  2. Capture and quarantine Files are downloaded from the publisher's own address and held apart until they have been checked for safety and integrity. Automated, logged
  3. Gate 1: preservation Files that pass the checks go into long-term storage in an open archival format (OCFL), together with a record of when, where from and how they were obtained. Automated, logged
  4. Gate 2: editorial acceptance A reviewer reads the material and decides what it says and how far it can be relied on. This is the only step where the archive starts to hold knowledge, as opposed to files. Human decision
  5. Gate 3: publication A separate decision, at a stated level of risk, about what is shown publicly and at what access level. Human decision

Published records will get permanent identifiers (ARKs, a long-lived identifier scheme used by libraries and archives), so that a citation made today still resolves decades from now.

Safeguards, and why the pace is deliberate

An evidence archive is only as good as its discipline, so we built and tested the rules before collecting anything. The code that enforces them is tested against a real database and real storage, and each test suite has been shown to fail when the rule it checks is broken.

What the archive will not do

  • No general web crawling. Material comes only from sources that have been registered one at a time. We will not harvest the open web or social media in bulk, either now or later.
  • No conclusions from collection. Collection creates no claims about anyone. Every collection run so far has produced zero conclusions, as designed.
  • No automatic profiling of people. Software never extracts personal data into structured records. A person is entered only by a reviewer, by hand.
  • Source ratings are for sorting only. A source's reliability grade helps decide what to review first. It never decides what is true.

A legal review first

Broader collection is paused until independent legal counsel has reviewed how the project handles personal data under European data-protection law. The question has been sent to counsel and we are waiting for the answer. Until the review is formally recorded, the archive collects only from individually approved sources. We chose to wait rather than risk mishandling anyone's data.

A legal home

The project has started forming a French non-profit association (an association loi 1901) to take on its long-term governance and responsibilities.

Civilian harm, war crimes and a memorial

On 2026-09-24 the project added a new subject area: civilian harm and war crimes committed in Russia's war against Ukraine. Alongside it we plan a memorial website for the people killed.

  • Every fact on the memorial comes from the archive. The memorial adds no facts of its own. Every detail links back to an archive record and the evidence behind it.
  • Incidents come first, then people. Each incident is recorded with each source's own count, and children are counted separately. The unnamed dead are always counted. A person is named only when a source names them, and only after a reviewer has checked the entry by hand.
  • No names are published before the legal review is recorded. After that, names are published only under the project's personal-data policy.
  • Remembering someone is not a legal verdict. Each record shows honestly whether a death is alleged to be a crime, under investigation, or established by a court.
  • The most sensitive areas wait for their own decisions. The deportation of children, sexual violence and torture will each need a separate, stricter decision before any material on them is collected.
  • Sources come in stages. First the UN monitoring mission and Ukraine's Prosecutor General, then regional administrations, then sources that name victims and existing memorial projects, which we will approach as partners.

A few terms

Source registration
The formal, recorded act of approving a specific publisher and address for collection. Without it, nothing is collected.
Quarantine
A holding area where downloaded files wait until safety and integrity checks are done.
Checksum (fixity)
A digital fingerprint of a file. If even one byte changes, the fingerprint changes, so stored files can be re-checked indefinitely.
OCFL
Oxford Common File Layout, an open standard for storing digital objects so that any future system can read them without special software.
Decision Record
A short document recording one decision, the options considered and the reasons for the choice. There are 110 so far.
Backfill
Going back through a channel's history to preserve posts made before collection began.
