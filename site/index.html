Ukraine's Second War of Independence — Project Briefing

A plain-language progress update on the evidence archive project, kept current alongside development.

Last updated: 2026-09-10

What this is

We are building a long-term evidence archive — documents, sanctions data, and other records connected to Russia's war against Ukraine and the networks that sustain it. The goal is a record that holds up years or decades from now: to courts, investigators, journalists, and historians, not just to us. This page is a plain-language progress update, kept current as the work develops.

Why the pace looks slow, on purpose

An evidence archive is only as good as its discipline. Before collecting anything at scale, we built and tested the rules for how evidence is captured, verified, and preserved — so that every record can be trusted, traced back to its source, and never quietly altered. That groundwork is now done and tested against a real database and real storage.

What has actually happened, as of today (2026-09-10)

  • Two sanctions lists — the EU's consolidated sanctions list and the US Treasury's OFAC list — are formally registered as sources.
  • The first real, live collection ran on 2026-09-09: five files, about 211 MB, pulled directly from the EU and US Treasury publishers, with zero failures.
  • Nothing was silently interpreted or asserted about anyone during that collection — by design, collecting data does not yet draw conclusions from it. That's a deliberate, later, human-reviewed step.

What's deliberately not done yet

  • Five more sanctions-list sources are identified and ready, but not yet turned on — each one is switched on individually, by a named human decision, not automatically.
  • Broader collection is paused until an external legal review of how we handle personal data is complete. We chose to wait rather than move fast and risk mishandling anyone's data.

What's next

Registering the remaining sanctions sources, commissioning that legal review, and continuing to expand what the archive can take in — always with the same rule: build the discipline first, then use it.
