## Context

`docs/legal/legal-review-brief.md` (deposited 2026-09-14, WP 3.4 Track A
item A6) found that POL-0001 §10 and WP 3.4 §7 both write "the
establishment jurisdiction" as though a country were already on record, and
none is: no controlled document names one. This blocks commissioning the
POL-0001 §10 external legal review outright — a reviewer and the applicable
law cannot be identified without it — so it was raised to the founder as
the lead item in README's "Open decisions for the next session" and put to
the founder directly, with three options (a formal legal entity in a named
country; the founder's own personal jurisdiction as an interim basis, no
entity existing yet; or leave it open for now).

The founder chose the second: **no formal legal entity exists yet; the
project's interim establishment jurisdiction is the founder's own personal
jurisdiction, France.**

## Alternatives considered

1. **Record France as the interim establishment jurisdiction, on the
   founder's own personal establishment, pending formation of a legal
   entity** (chosen — the founder's direct answer).
2. Wait to name any jurisdiction until a formal legal entity is
   incorporated. Rejected by the founder's choice: it would leave POL-0001
   §10 permanently unable to be commissioned, since forming an entity is a
   larger and slower undertaking than naming an interim jurisdiction for
   an already-operating project.
3. Leave the question open and work on a different Track A item. Not
   chosen; the founder answered directly instead.

## Decision

1. **The project's interim establishment jurisdiction is France** — the
   founder/principal editor's own personal jurisdiction, in the absence of
   a separate incorporated or registered legal entity. This is explicitly
   **interim**: it names where the project is presently established for
   the purpose of identifying applicable law and counsel, not a claim that
   a formal entity exists or that incorporation in France is settled policy
   for the future.
2. **POL-0001 is amended to v1.1** to name France in §10, replacing the
   unnamed "its establishment jurisdiction" with the concrete fact, and
   noting the entity gap so a reviewer sees it rather than assumes a
   registered organization exists. No other substantive change to POL-0001;
   this is a factual amendment, not a re-ruling of any §8 position.
3. **`docs/legal/legal-review-brief.md` is updated**: §2's project summary
   now states France directly (GDPR applying as directly-applicable EU law,
   CNIL as the relevant supervisory authority, France's implementing statute
   for Art. 85 member-state provisions as a live question for the reviewer
   itself); §4's "blocks commissioning" list drops the jurisdiction item as
   resolved, and gains a new item — whether the review should address
   *entity formation* as a preliminary question, since GDPR's establishment
   concept ordinarily presumes some organized controller, not only a
   private individual running a project.
4. **This does not choose a reviewer or commission the review.** Those
   remain open (README "Open decisions"), now narrower: a named firm or
   individual in France, and whether entity formation is itself the first
   question to ask them.
5. **POL-0001 §9's releases remain suspended** — naming the jurisdiction
   removes one blocker to commissioning the review; it is not the review
   itself, and DR-0072's operative limit is unchanged until the review's
   outcome is recorded.

## Consequences

1. The A6 legal-review brief can now be completed to the point of being
   ready to send, once a reviewer is chosen — the one blocking gap it found
   is resolved.
2. **A new, smaller open question surfaces**: whether the project should
   form a legal entity before or as part of commissioning the review, since
   "establishment" under GDPR ordinarily presumes an organized controller.
   This is now recorded as an open question rather than silently assumed
   either way.
3. POL-0001's version history now shows a v1.1 amendment distinct from its
   v1.0 approval, per DR-0046's controlled-document conventions — tracked in
   `docs/policies/README.md`'s provenance table with both hashes.
