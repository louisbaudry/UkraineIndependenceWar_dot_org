# DR-0044 — Territorial-status vocabulary

**Category:** architecture / legal | **Status:** Approved | **Decided:** 2026-08-11 by founder/principal editor
**Origin:** CDR-W6-7, WP 0.7 §4/§6 | **Supersedes:** — (fulfills the joint item assigned by DR-0015) | **Superseded by:** —

## Context

Record §46: sovereignty, territorial claim, administration, occupation, and de
facto control are separate relationships. DR-0015 assigned their vocabulary to
joint WS2/WS6 development, grounded in the CRM period concept.

## Alternatives considered

1. Typed temporal relations grounded in period phenomena (chosen).
2. Territory attributes on places (rejected: statuses are relations between
   actors and territory over time, with contested variants — attributes cannot
   carry contestation).

## Decision

Territorial statuses are **typed, evidence-backed temporal relations** between
actors and territorial extents, grounded in period phenomena (an occupation is
a spatiotemporal phenomenon, CRM E4 pattern): **sovereignty claim,
international recognition/non-recognition, administration, occupation, de facto
control** — each with time-spans, evidence, and asserting parties where the
status is a legal characterization (a UN General Assembly non-recognition
resolution is an authority-attributed assertion, §62). Competing
characterizations of the same territory coexist under the epistemic layer
(§40); geometry attaches per DR-0010's place model with temporal validity.

## Consequences

- Crimea-type cases are expressible without the system taking silent sides:
  the project's own conclusions are explicit project assertions (§33).
- Front-line/control mapping over time becomes a sequence of evidence-backed
  relations, not overwritten geometry.
- DR-0015's joint-vocabulary obligation is discharged.
