# DR-0024 — Adopt the six-layer epistemic architecture

**Category:** epistemology / architecture | **Status:** Approved | **Decided:** 2026-08-11 by founder/principal editor
**Origin:** CDR-W4-1, WP 0.5 §5/§10 | **Supersedes:** — | **Superseded by:** —

## Context

Record §31 proposed a provisional six-layer epistemic structure and required it
to be tested against established theory before adoption. WP 0.5's discipline
survey (intelligence analysis, IPCC uncertainty practice, legal evidence,
source criticism) confirmed the shape.

## Alternatives considered

1. Six-layer architecture as refined in WP 0.5 §5 (chosen).
2. Two-layer model (facts + sources) (rejected: collapses documentary/world/
   project distinctions the record forbids collapsing, §32–33).
3. Defer until argumentation study (rejected: layers 1–5 are independent of the
   layer-6 formalism choice).

## Decision

The epistemic structure of the knowledge system is:

1. **World layer** — entities/events (DR-0010): what propositions are about.
2. **Documentary assertions** — what sources say, anchored to exact passages
   (DR-0017/0018), owned by the source, entering the graph by adoption.
3. **Evidence relations** — explicit, claim-relative support/contradict/bears-on
   links between sources/observations and propositions (§29).
4. **Project assertions** — beliefs held by the project or named analysts,
   produced by visible inference, under human accountability (§79).
5. **Epistemic assessments** — versioned status/likelihood/confidence attached
   to assertions; prior states are never rewritten (§63).
6. **Arguments** — inference/defeater structure (formalism per Workstream 5).

Being in the corpus never implies being evidentially used (Principle 5).

## Consequences

- Every downstream vocabulary (assessments, arguments, publication) binds to a
  named layer.
- "The source says X," "X happened," and "we conclude X" are permanently
  distinct objects.
