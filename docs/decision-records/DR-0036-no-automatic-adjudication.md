# DR-0036 — No automatic adjudication of arguments

**Category:** epistemology / editorial | **Status:** Approved | **Decided:** 2026-08-11 by founder/principal editor
**Origin:** CDR-W5-5, WP 0.6 §3/§6 | **Supersedes:** — | **Superseded by:** —

## Context

Formal argumentation semantics (Dung extensions and successors) can compute
argument acceptability. Record §79 requires human accountability for
consequential conclusions; §40 forbids mechanical resolution of
contradictions; DR-0027 barred grades from determining truth.

## Alternatives considered

1. Formal semantics as analytic aids only (chosen).
2. Computed acceptability determining conclusion status (rejected: violates
   §79 and §40; an algorithm would become the editor).
3. Ban formal analysis entirely (rejected: flagging inconsistencies and
   unanswered defeaters is genuinely useful and harmless as advice).

## Decision

**No computation determines a project conclusion.** Formal acceptability
semantics, consistency checking, and defeater-coverage analysis may run as
**analytic aids** — surfacing unanswered critical questions, unattacked
assumptions, or inconsistent evidence — but their outputs are advisory inputs
to human judgment. Consequential conclusions are human inference-making
records (DR-0031) with visible, attackable structure. The same rule applies to
AI-generated argument analysis (§79: AI proposes; humans remain accountable).

## Consequences

- Tooling may rank, flag, and warn; it may never conclude.
- The rule is testable: every published consequential conclusion traces to a
  human inference-making record, not to an algorithm's output.
