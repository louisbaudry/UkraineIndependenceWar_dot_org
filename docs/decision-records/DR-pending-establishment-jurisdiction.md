## Context

[POL-0001](../policies/POL-0001-personal-data.md) §10 requires external legal
review "in its establishment jurisdiction" before collection at scale
touching living private individuals, and suspends §9's release of the
DR-0071 interim constraints until that review is recorded. The review is
therefore the single gate in front of the whole acquisition plan
([WP 3.4](../phase-3/working-papers/wp-3.4-foundational-corpus-acquisition.md)
Track B).

**No document in this repository states what that jurisdiction is.** POL-0001
§2 says only that the project is "European-based". Nor does any document
state whether the controller is the founder personally or a legal entity —
the Phase I record contemplates an incorporated legal entity for the
project's institutional continuity, but none is recorded as existing — nor
where the archive server is hosted, though every registration and collection
record refers to it.

This surfaced while drafting the A6 legal-review brief
([`docs/legal/legal-review-brief.md`](../legal/legal-review-brief.md),
2026-09-15). Five of that brief's eleven questions — Q1 (Art. 89 basis and
safeguards), Q3 (the applicable Art. 85 provisions), Q5 (retention), Q7
(archiving and research exceptions, the sui generis database right) and Q9
(`robots.txt`'s legal weight) — have no law to be answered in until the
jurisdiction is fixed, and **no counsel can be selected without it**. Q6
(DPIA, DPO, Art. 27 representative) cannot be answered without knowing who
the controller is. The hosting location bears on establishment and on
transfer analysis.

The gap is not a drafting oversight in POL-0001. The policy presumes these
facts because they are the kind of thing a project normally knows about
itself; this one has simply never written them down. They are load-bearing
legal facts, and settling them as a by-product of signing an engagement
letter would leave the project's most consequential legal premises
undocumented.

## Alternatives considered

Three were put to the founder on 2026-09-15.

1. **Record all three facts in one Decision Record now** — jurisdiction,
   controller form, and hosting location — before any counsel is briefed.
   Cleanest record. Rejected: it forces the entity question now, and if the
   founder intends to incorporate later but has not chosen where, the record
   would carry a placeholder dressed as a decision.

2. **Record the establishment jurisdiction and the hosting location now,
   with the controller stated as the founder as a natural person**, and note
   explicitly that later incorporation is a "material change to … 
   jurisdiction" under POL-0001 §11 which triggers a recorded review
   (**chosen**). Smallest commitment the founder has to stand behind; records
   what is true today rather than what is intended; the policy already has
   the machinery for the later change. Accepted cost: if an entity is later
   formed in a different jurisdiction, a second and probably smaller legal
   spend may be needed.

3. **Commission counsel first and have them advise on jurisdiction and form
   together.** Rejected as circular: counsel qualified in a jurisdiction
   cannot be selected before the jurisdiction is named, and Q3's Art. 85
   analysis is unanswerable without it. It also does not terminate — the
   question would return in the same shape after the fee was spent.

## Decision

1. **The project's establishment jurisdiction is `[TO BE SUPPLIED BY THE
   FOUNDER]`.** This is the jurisdiction in which the external legal review
   POL-0001 §10 requires is obtained, and the law against which POL-0001 is
   tested.

2. **The controller, for data-protection purposes, is the founder/principal
   editor as a natural person.** No legal entity exists for the project, and
   none is assumed. This is a statement of what is true as at 2026-09-15, not
   a commitment to remain unincorporated.

3. **The archive server is hosted `[TO BE SUPPLIED BY THE FOUNDER — provider
   and country]`.**

4. **Incorporation, or a change of either the establishment jurisdiction or
   the archive server's hosting country, is a material change under
   POL-0001 §11** ("reviewed … on any material change to collection scope,
   jurisdiction, or applicable law") and triggers a recorded policy review.
   Where the §10 legal review has by then been obtained, that review's
   continued applicability is reassessed as part of it, and the reassessment
   is recorded whether or not it changes anything.

5. **This record is the source of these facts.** The A6 brief's §9.1–9.3
   blanks are filled *from* this record; the brief is not where the facts are
   decided. Any later correction happens by superseding this record, never by
   editing the brief.

## What is still missing

## Consequences

1. **A6's §9.1–9.3 blanks close when Decisions 1 and 3 are filled.** §9.4
   (whether counsel is asked about the project as it is or as it intends to
   be) and §9.5 (one engagement or two, given that Part B is copyright and
   platform-terms work a data-protection practice may decline) remain open
   founder decisions and are untouched by this record.

2. **Counsel can be selected** once Decision 1 is filled. Selecting counsel
   is not commissioning; commissioning the review is a separate founder act,
   and this record authorises neither.

3. **Q6 becomes answerable but not simpler.** A natural-person controller
   does not avoid the DPIA question, and may make the DPO and Art. 27
   representative questions sharper rather than moot. Counsel is asked
   about the controller as recorded at Decision 2, and told at the same time
   that incorporation is contemplated — otherwise the advice is answering a
   question the project may stop asking.

4. **A risk is accepted, deliberately.** If an entity is later formed in a
   jurisdiction other than Decision 1's, part of the review may need
   redoing. Decision 4 is the mechanism that catches it; the alternative was
   deciding the entity question now, which the founder declined for good
   reason.

5. **CDR-P3-42 is discharged by this record** and the brief's §11 says so.
   CDR-P3-43 (what "the review's outcome is recorded" means) and CDR-P3-44
   (a rights position marked unreviewed cannot reach Gate 3) remain
   candidates, untouched.

6. **LEGAL-009 stays "partially satisfied."** A recorded jurisdiction is not
   a recorded review, and this record moves nothing in
   [REQ-LEGAL](../requirements/REQ-LEGAL-legal-layer.md). Collection stays
   bound by DR-0071's interim constraints.

7. **Nothing about collection changes.** No source becomes registrable, no
   run becomes authorised, and Track B does not start. This record fixes
   facts about the project, not about its archive.
